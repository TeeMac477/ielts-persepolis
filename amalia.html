Part 1 Strategy Guide

What is Part 1?

The examiner asks you 4–6 questions on 2–3 familiar topics (work, home, hobbies, etc.). It lasts 4–5 minutes. This is a warm-up — your answers should be natural, not rehearsed speeches.

The Golden Rule: Answer + Extend

Never give a one-word answer. Use this formula:

  • Direct answer (1 sentence) — answer the question clearly
  • Extension (1–2 sentences) — add a reason, example, or contrast

Bad: "Do you like reading?" → "Yes, I do."

Good: "Yes, I'm quite keen on reading. I tend to read fiction before bed because it helps me unwind after a long day."

Key Phrases to Sound Natural

FunctionPhrases
Giving opinionsI'd say... / I suppose... / I reckon... / To be honest... / Personally...
Adding reasonsmainly because... / the reason is... / that's partly due to...
Giving examplesfor instance... / such as... / like... / a good example would be...
Contrastingalthough... / having said that... / on the other hand... / mind you...
Frequencyfrom time to time / on a regular basis / every now and then / hardly ever
HedgingI suppose... / it depends on... / not necessarily... / more or less...
Showing enthusiasmI'm really into... / I'm passionate about... / I'm a big fan of...
Time markersthese days / back when I was younger / over the years / recently

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-answering — Part 1 answers should be 2–4 sentences, not a 1-minute monologue
  • Memorised scripts — examiners notice immediately; it lowers your score
  • "Yes/No" only — always extend with a reason or example
  • Repeating the question word-for-word — paraphrase: "Do you like sport?" → "I'm quite into sport, actually..."
  • Using "I think" for every answer — vary it: I suppose, I'd say, I reckon, in my view
  • Flat intonation — show interest in what you're saying

Example Answers

What is your job?
I work as a marketing coordinator at a tech company. I'm mainly in charge of managing our social media campaigns and coordinating with the design team.
Do you like your job?
On the whole, yes — I find it quite rewarding because I get to be creative. Having said that, it can be rather demanding during peak seasons when we have tight deadlines.
Would you change your job?
To be honest, I'm fairly content with what I'm doing at the moment. But if I had the chance, I'd love to move into UX design because I'm really into understanding how people interact with products.

Tips for "Work" questions

  • Don't just name your job — say what you actually do day to day
  • Use "work as" not "work like" — "I work as a teacher" (not "like a teacher")
  • For hypothetical questions (would you change?), use conditionals: "If I had the chance, I'd..."

Practice Plan & Improvement Tips

Daily Practice Routine (15–20 minutes)

  • Pick a topic card above and answer all the questions out loud. Time yourself — aim for 15–30 seconds per answer.
  • Record yourself on your phone. Listen back and check: Did you extend? Did you use any phrases from the word bank? Was your grammar accurate?
  • Learn 3 new phrases from the word banks each day. Use them in sentences about your life.
  • Shadow a native speaker — find a YouTube video of someone answering Part 1 questions and repeat what they say, copying their intonation.

Weekly Activities

  • Monday & Thursday: Practise 2 topic cards (answer all questions aloud + record)
  • Tuesday & Friday: Review word banks — create example sentences using 5 new phrases
  • Wednesday: Listen to a podcast or news in English for 15 minutes. Note any useful vocabulary.
  • Saturday: Do a full mock Part 1 — ask someone to read you random questions from 3 different topics
  • Sunday: Watch an English film or series (with English subtitles) and note 5 natural expressions

Self-Assessment Checklist

After each practice, ask yourself:

  • Did I answer the question directly? (not go off-topic)
  • Did I extend with a reason, example, or contrast?
  • Did I use at least 2 advanced phrases? (not just "I think")
  • Did I avoid repeating the question word-for-word?
  • Was my answer 2–4 sentences? (not too short, not too long)
  • Did I sound natural and confident? (not rehearsed)
  • Did I use a variety of tenses correctly?

Useful Resources

  • Cambridge Dictionary Online — check pronunciation and example sentences
  • BBC Learning English — free videos and podcasts for natural English
  • IELTS Liz / IELTS Simon — sample answers and examiner tips for each part
  • Podcasts: "6 Minute English" (BBC), "All Ears English" — great for picking up idioms
  • Shadowing technique: Watch TED Talks with subtitles, pause after each sentence, repeat aloud

Pronunciation Tips

  • Word stress matters: "phoPHOgraphy" not "photoGRAPHy" — wrong stress confuses listeners
  • Connected speech: Practise linking words together naturally: "sort of" → "sortuv", "kind of" → "kinda"
  • Intonation: Rise at the end of yes/no questions, fall at the end of statements. Avoid flat tone.
  • Contractions: Use them! "I'm", "I'd", "I've", "I'll" sound more natural than full forms
  • Fillers: Instead of "um" or "uh", use: "well...", "let me think...", "that's a good question..."
