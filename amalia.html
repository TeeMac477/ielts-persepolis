Part 1 Strategy Guide

What is Part 1?

The examiner asks you 4–6 questions on 2–3 familiar topics (work, home, hobbies, etc.). It lasts 4–5 minutes. This is a warm-up — your answers should be natural, not rehearsed speeches.

The Golden Rule: Answer + Extend

Never give a one-word answer. Use this formula:

  • Direct answer (1 sentence) — answer the question clearly
  • Extension (1–2 sentences) — add a reason, example, or contrast

Bad: "Do you like reading?" → "Yes, I do."

Good: "Yes, I'm quite keen on reading. I tend to read fiction before bed because it helps me unwind after a long day."

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-answering — Part 1 answers should be 2–4 sentences, not a 1-minute monologue
  • Memorised scripts — examiners notice immediately; it lowers your score
  • "Yes/No" only — always extend with a reason or example
  • Repeating the question word-for-word — paraphrase: "Do you like sport?" → "I'm quite into sport, actually..."
  • Using "I think" for every answer — vary it: I suppose, I'd say, I reckon, in my view
  • Flat intonation — show interest in what you're saying

Example Answers

What is your job?
I work as a marketing coordinator at a tech company. I'm mainly in charge of managing our social media campaigns and coordinating with the design team.
Do you like your job?
On the whole, yes — I find it quite rewarding because I get to be creative. Having said that, it can be rather demanding during peak seasons when we have tight deadlines.
Would you change your job?
To be honest, I'm fairly content with what I'm doing at the moment. But if I had the chance, I'd love to move into UX design because I'm really into understanding how people interact with products.

Tips for "Work" questions

  • Don't just name your job — say what you actually do day to day
  • Use "work as" not "work like" — "I work as a teacher" (not "like a teacher")
  • For hypothetical questions (would you change?), use conditionals: "If I had the chance, I'd..."

Practice Plan & Improvement Tips

Daily Practice Routine (15–20 minutes)

  • Pick a topic card above and answer all the questions out loud. Time yourself — aim for 15–30 seconds per answer.
  • Record yourself on your phone. Listen back and check: Did you extend? Did you use any phrases from the word bank? Was your grammar accurate?
  • Learn 3 new phrases from the word banks each day. Use them in sentences about your life.
  • Shadow a native speaker — find a YouTube video of someone answering Part 1 questions and repeat what they say, copying their intonation.

Weekly Activities

  • Monday & Thursday: Practise 2 topic cards (answer all questions aloud + record)
  • Tuesday & Friday: Review word banks — create example sentences using 5 new phrases
  • Wednesday: Listen to a podcast or news in English for 15 minutes. Note any useful vocabulary.
  • Saturday: Do a full mock Part 1 — ask someone to read you random questions from 3 different topics
  • Sunday: Watch an English film or series (with English subtitles) and note 5 natural expressions

Self-Assessment Checklist

After each practice, ask yourself:

  • Did I answer the question directly? (not go off-topic)
  • Did I extend with a reason, example, or contrast?
  • Did I use at least 2 advanced phrases? (not just "I think")
  • Did I avoid repeating the question word-for-word?
  • Was my answer 2–4 sentences? (not too short, not too long)
  • Did I sound natural and confident? (not rehearsed)
  • Did I use a variety of tenses correctly?

Task 1 Structure Templates

Use these templates for every Task 1 type. Each follows the same 4-paragraph pattern. Используй эти шаблоны для каждого типа Task 1. Все следуют одной и той же 4-параграфной структуре.

Line Graph Structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction (1–2 sentences)

“The line graph illustrates/compares [what is measured] in [where/what categories] between [start year] and [end year].”

Paragraph 2 — Overview (2 sentences, NO figures)

“Overall, [main upward/downward trend]. [Second pattern or notable feature, e.g., two lines converged/diverged].”

Paragraph 3 — Body 1 (upward trends or group A)

Describe lines that go UP. Use: rose sharply, climbed steadily, increased from X to Y, peaked at Z in [year], surged.

Paragraph 4 — Body 2 (downward trends or group B)

Describe lines that go DOWN. Use: declined gradually, fell from X to Y, dropped to a low of Z, dipped before recovering.

Группируй линии по направлению (вверх / вниз), а не описывай каждую отдельно слева направо.

Bar Chart Structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction

“The bar chart shows/compares [what] across [categories] in [year/period].”

Paragraph 2 — Overview (NO figures)

“Overall, [category X] had the highest [measure], while [category Y] recorded the lowest. [Any notable pattern].”

Paragraph 3 — Body 1 (highest/largest bars)

Describe the top 2–3 bars with figures. Compare between them.

Paragraph 4 — Body 2 (smallest/remaining bars)

Describe the remaining bars. Highlight any that are equal or very close.

Для столбчатых диаграмм: группируй по размеру (крупные вместе, мелкие вместе), а не описывай слева направо.

Pie Chart Structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction

“The pie chart(s) illustrate(s) the proportion of [what] in [where/when].”

Paragraph 2 — Overview (NO figures)

“Overall, [largest category] dominated, accounting for the majority. [Any identical/similar values between charts].”

Paragraph 3 — Body 1 (dominant slices)

The largest 2–3 slices with exact percentages. Use: comprised, accounted for, represented, made up.

Paragraph 4 — Body 2 (smaller slices + comparisons)

Remaining slices grouped together. Add comparisons between charts if there are two.

Your specific fix: Do NOT use “accounts for” more than once. Rotate through: comprised, represented, stood at, made up, constituted.

Table Structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction

“The table presents data on [what] for [categories] in [years/places].”

Paragraph 2 — Overview (NO figures)

“Overall, [highest category] ranked first in all/most periods, while [lowest] remained at the bottom.”

Body Paragraphs

Group rows/columns by similarity. Select 4–6 key figures only. Do NOT describe every single cell.

Для таблиц: не описывай каждую ячейку! Выбери 4–6 ключевых цифр и группируй по сходству.

Process Diagram Structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction + Overview

“The diagram illustrates how [product] is [made/produced]. Overall, it is a [linear/cyclical] process consisting of [number] stages, beginning with [first] and ending with [last].”

Paragraph 2 — Body 1 (first half of stages)

Use passive voice: is harvested, is transported, is heated, are mixed. Add purpose: in order to, so that, which enables. Sequencing: First, Next, Then, Subsequently, Following this.

Paragraph 3 — Body 2 (second half of stages)

Continue with passive voice. End with the final product. Use: Once completed, At the final stage, Finally.

You’re strong here! Your process exercises scored very well. Keep using passive voice and purpose phrases.

Map / Plan Structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction + Overview

“The two maps compare [place] in [year 1] and [year 2]. Overall, the area underwent significant development, with [biggest change].”

Paragraph 2 — Body 1 (map 1 / northern area)

Describe the earlier map or one area. Use past passive: was demolished, was replaced by, were constructed.

Paragraph 3 — Body 2 (map 2 / southern area)

Describe the later map or other area. Use direction words: to the north/south/east/west of, adjacent to, in the centre.

Для карт используй прошедшее время в пассиве (was built, were removed) и слова направления (to the north of, in the south-west corner).
