Section 3 — Grammar Focus

The Passive Voice

Passive voice is one of the most important grammatical structures for IELTS Band 7+. It appears in Task 1 process descriptions, Task 2 academic arguments, and Speaking. Examiners explicitly reward it as evidence of grammatical range.

What Is Passive Voice?

In active voice, the subject performs the action:
Satrapi wrote the book. (Satrapi = subject; does the writing)

In passive voice, the subject receives the action:
The book was written by Satrapi. (The book = subject; receives the writing)

The agent (who did it) can be omitted when it is unknown, unimportant, or obvious:
The book was published in 2000. (We don't need to say "by the publisher")

The Formula

Subject  +  be (correct tense)  +  past participle  +  (by + agent)

The verb be carries the tense. The main verb is always in past participle form (written, published, introduced, forced, believed…).

When to Use Passive Voice in IELTS

  • Task 1 — Process diagrams: "The material is heated to 400°C, then is cooled rapidly."
  • Task 2 — Academic argument: "It is widely argued that technology has transformed modern education."
  • When the agent is unknown: "The law was changed in 1979." (we don't need to say who changed it)
  • When the agent is obvious: "The suspect was arrested." (obviously by police)
  • To sound more formal: "Mistakes were made" is more formal than "They made mistakes."
  • To vary sentence structure and demonstrate grammatical range to the examiner.

Note for Russian Speakers

Russian uses passive voice less frequently than English, preferring reflexive constructions (-ся / -сь): Книга была написана (passive) vs книга написана or книга пишется (reflexive).

In English academic writing, passive voice is strongly preferred and sounds more formal than active. You should consciously practice building passive sentences until they feel natural.

Common Russian-speaker errors:

  • Using active where passive is expected: "People widely believe that…" → "It is widely believed that…"
  • Wrong past participle: "was wrote" → "was written"
  • Omitting be: "The book published in 2000" → "The book was published in 2000"
  • Wrong form of be: "The laws is changed" → "The laws are changed"

13 — Perfect Tenses

All Perfect Tense Forms — Explanation & Exercises

Why perfect tenses matter for IELTS:
Band 7+ requires a range of complex structures used accurately. Perfect tenses show the examiner you can express relationships between time periods — not just "what happened" but "what had happened before," "what has been happening since," and "what will have happened by."

1. PRESENT PERFECT SIMPLE

have / has + past participle
Use for: actions with present relevance, unfinished time periods, experiences
Signals: since, for, already, yet, just, recently, so far, this week/month/year, ever, never
Active: Scientists have discovered a new species.
Passive: A new species has been discovered (by scientists).
Common error: "The report was published this year" → "The report has been published this year" (unfinished time)

2. PRESENT PERFECT CONTINUOUS

have / has + been + -ing
Use for: actions that started in the past and are still continuing, or recently finished with visible results
Signals: since, for, all day/week, how long, lately, recently
Example: Researchers have been studying this phenomenon for decades.
Example: The government has been investing heavily in renewable energy since 2015.
vs Simple: "I have read the report" (finished) vs "I have been reading the report" (still reading or just stopped)

3. PAST PERFECT SIMPLE

had + past participle
Use for: an action completed BEFORE another past action ("the past of the past")
Signals: before, after, by the time, already, until, when (+ past simple)
Active: The government had already introduced the policy before the crisis began.
Passive: The policy had already been introduced before the crisis began.
IELTS use: "By 2010, the number of users had reached 50 million." (Writing Task 1 — describing data at a point in the past)

4. PAST PERFECT CONTINUOUS

had + been + -ing
Use for: a continuous action that was happening BEFORE another past event
Signals: for, since, before, when, by the time, all day
Example: Scientists had been warning about climate change for years before governments took action.
Example: She had been living in Moscow for ten years when she decided to move.
IELTS Speaking: "Before I started university, I had been working as a translator for two years."

5. FUTURE PERFECT SIMPLE

will + have + past participle
Use for: an action that will be completed BEFORE a future time
Signals: by (+ future time), by the time, before, by then
Active: By 2030, the population will have exceeded 9 billion.
Passive: By 2030, the project will have been completed.
IELTS Writing: "By the end of this century, sea levels will have risen by up to one metre."

6. FUTURE PERFECT CONTINUOUS

will + have + been + -ing
Use for: the duration of an action up to a future point
Signals: by, for, by the time
Example: By next year, she will have been studying English for six years.
Example: By 2030, this city will have been growing for three consecutive decades.
