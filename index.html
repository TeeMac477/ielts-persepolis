Section 3 — Grammar Focus

The Passive Voice

Passive voice is one of the most important grammatical structures for IELTS Band 7+. It appears in Task 1 process descriptions, Task 2 academic arguments, and Speaking. Examiners explicitly reward it as evidence of grammatical range.

What Is Passive Voice?

In active voice, the subject performs the action:
Satrapi wrote the book. (Satrapi = subject; does the writing)

In passive voice, the subject receives the action:
The book was written by Satrapi. (The book = subject; receives the writing)

The agent (who did it) can be omitted when it is unknown, unimportant, or obvious:
The book was published in 2000. (We don't need to say "by the publisher")

The Formula

Subject  +  be (correct tense)  +  past participle  +  (by + agent)

The verb be carries the tense. The main verb is always in past participle form (written, published, introduced, forced, believed…).

Passive in Every Tense

When to Use Passive Voice in IELTS

  • Task 1 — Process diagrams: "The material is heated to 400°C, then is cooled rapidly."
  • Task 2 — Academic argument: "It is widely argued that technology has transformed modern education."
  • When the agent is unknown: "The law was changed in 1979." (we don't need to say who changed it)
  • When the agent is obvious: "The suspect was arrested." (obviously by police)
  • To sound more formal: "Mistakes were made" is more formal than "They made mistakes."
  • To vary sentence structure and demonstrate grammatical range to the examiner.

Note for Russian Speakers

Russian uses passive voice less frequently than English, preferring reflexive constructions (-ся / -сь): Книга была написана (passive) vs книга написана or книга пишется (reflexive).

In English academic writing, passive voice is strongly preferred and sounds more formal than active. You should consciously practice building passive sentences until they feel natural.

Common Russian-speaker errors:

  • Using active where passive is expected: "People widely believe that…" → "It is widely believed that…"
  • Wrong past participle: "was wrote" → "was written"
  • Omitting be: "The book published in 2000" → "The book was published in 2000"
  • Wrong form of be: "The laws is changed" → "The laws are changed"

Exercise C
Find and Correct the Error

Each sentence contains one passive voice error. Identify it and write the correction.

1
"The book was wrote by Satrapi in French."
2
"The revolution is describing in great detail throughout the novel."
3
"She was force to leave her country."
4
"The results have been announced by the committee yesterday."
5
"It is believe that education is the key to success."
1.  wrote → written. Was + past participle. "Wrote" is simple past active; the passive needs the p.p. "written."
2.  describing → described. Passive = be + past participle. "Describing" is the present participle (used in active continuous).
3.  force → forced. Past participle of "force" is "forced." Missing -d.
4.  have been announced → were announced. "Yesterday" is a definite past time reference — present perfect cannot be used with it. Use past simple passive.
5.  believe → believed. It is + past participle. "Believe" is the base form; passive needs "believed."
